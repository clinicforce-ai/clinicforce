Use the right store for the question. A database row describes a record; a file store holds its original bytes.

Original PDFs, insurance card images and other document files.

A service for FHIR, the standard format used to exchange structured health data.

Outside providers · evidence

Understand insurance results

ClinicForce has three insurance workflows. Read the result according to the evidence that produced it.

V1 · read documents

Extract and review facts from uploaded files. This does not establish live eligibility.

V2 · ask a payer

Submit a transaction, then follow its pending, complete or error state.

Commercial · estimate a visit

Combine the visit, insurance benefits and verified clinic rates to calculate an estimate.

Unknown patient liability is not $0. A $0 check-in advance can be a documented collection decision while the final insured balance remains unknown. Missing evidence or a failed request stays visible; a saved case or insurance card alone does not prove coverage.

Commercial visit, step by step
  1. Identify the visit. GET /api/doctor/insurance/commercial/appointments reads the clinic's eCW appointment context. Patient, appointment, coverage, provider, location, service date, and planned codes must be reconciled.
  2. Prepare the case. POST /appointments/readiness reports missing inputs. POST /appointments/previsit saves or reuses a case. A unique supported policy continues automatically. Two or more policy or payer suggestions appear in optional dropdowns; estimation does not wait for selection. An unresolved UHC payer result remains on the insurance path unless the clinician explicitly chooses cash.
  3. Check coverage. An existing eCW eligibility result may be reused; otherwise configured Stedi may perform eligibility. Discovery can return a payer 271; use it only after verifying identity, coverage dates and required service categories. An uncertain or pending request must not be blindly repeated.
  4. Price only with evidence. Match a verified payer connection, provider network, active rate version, CPT/HCPCS, place of service, and effective date. visit_estimate.price_lines() separates insured amount, conditional planning estimate, and cash option.
  5. Preserve history. insurance_cases.workflow_json stores working state; insurance_case_events.snapshot_json freezes eligible historical results. GET /commercial/history is eCW visit history; saved checks and later 835/financial reads are separate concepts.

Shared service planning and clinical review. The versioned classification in cpt_rules.SERVICE_FAMILIES supplies service family, role, planning group and initial eligibility categories for office, nerve, diagnostic and chemodenervation candidates. It supplies no payer price or approval. eCW scheduled codes, completed-visit history and Bedrock recommendations remain preview choices until the clinician explicitly selects service IDs and quantities; a preview cannot authorize a CPT-specific insured advance. A separately supported fixed office copay may be collected once across unresolved E/M alternatives when its own identity, network, deductible and requirement conditions pass. The selected lines use the same price_lines, requirement evaluation and collection_guidance path for all services. A Botox booking or received vial does not select a drug, injection procedure or separate E/M code. The clinician records chart or order evidence, indication, anatomy, drug brand and supply, planned versus performed state, reviewed modifiers and any separate E/M work. A future visit uses proposed HCPCS units without requiring administered dose. A completed visit requires performed evidence. Documented waste never increases billable units automatically: discarded units leave drug pricing unresolved until payer-specific waste coding and any separate claim lines are reviewed. botox_review.py retains only the specialized clinical checks.

Each selected medical line uses its own procedure-specific or payer-confirmed service-category benefit; an office copay does not price a drug or injection. The common LineEvidenceReview form draws available categories from saved payer rows and can record benefit, referral and authorization evidence for any selected service. The UI and backend reject a selected broad category unrelated to the line, including chiropractic STC 33 for a botulinum drug or chemodenervation service; an exact procedure row can be reviewed separately. The pricing path rechecks saved selections so an older invalid review cannot become a priced line on replay. STC 1 is general medical care and still requires a documented, member-specific applicability decision before its cost share can price a selected drug or procedure; it is not automatic proof of drug coverage. Deterministically applicable saved benefits continue through automatic normalization without a routine manual row choice. Staff review unresolved deductible treatment, exact product/provider participation, scoped allowance or member-specific authorization when electronic evidence is insufficient. An approved defer-to-claim policy may record $0 due today while liability remains unknown. A later empty discovery response does not erase saved active eligibility. A current patient-bound eCW eligibility response with a common office copay can be reused as planning evidence while clinician approval is pending, avoiding a new paid check solely because collection is withheld. See Stedi service type codes, UHC commercial medical drug policies and UHC member-specific requirement lookup; public guidance is not member approval.

For an in-person office visit, shared Stedi row selection excludes STC 98 rows whose benefit messages limit them to telemedicine, applied behavior analysis, or agents, drugs and supplies, even when the payer omits a place-of-service code. The raw rows remain visible as reported options and their evidence paths are retained as excluded service evidence. A specialist qualifier does not erase a different service limitation in the same message. Authorization indicators are evaluated separately from financial copay equality: two otherwise equal applicable copay rows do not conflict solely because their authorization metadata differs. A requirement attached to an excluded service does not become the office visit's authorization rule.

Published FEP PSHB benefits. For a patient-bound 2026 eligibility response, the commercial flow identifies FEP Blue Basic PSHB only when the BASIC display label, 33A enrollment code, FEP source marker, and R-prefix member number agree. The identified product stays separate from a clinic's Blue Choice PPO plan alias and fee schedule. The 2026 OPM PSHB brochure establishes no annual Basic medical deductible and, for an office visit with a verified FEP Preferred rendering provider, a $50 specialist copay. The doctor-facing arrival panel displays this published $50 Preferred benefit with its OPM source and explicit conditions even when the collect-now amount is unresolved; it never labels the published benefit as an approved collection amount. The fixed copay can support a limited check-in advance across conditional established office E/M alternatives without a final CPT allowance only when there is no conflicting member-specific benefit, other primary coverage or service issue and the payer route has an administrator-approved collect_verified_copay policy. It is not a final claim liability or a substitute for clinician coding. A Preferred benefit label alone does not establish the provider's participation; an exact FEP contract or provider-specific payer statement must do that. FEP referral and office E/M authorization research uses the dated OPM rule separately from member-specific approval evidence. The Clinical Library's ordinary Blue Choice PPO rate source cannot price an FEP allowance. See the 2026 OPM PSHB brochure and the FEP Preferred provider finder.

V2 Stedi bridge safeguards. The temporary demo V2 Stedi path uses a clinic matrix row only when one returned plan name matches the configured payer, provider NPI, place of service and date. Opposing matching rows or multiple returned plan names leave participation undetermined with the precise conflict stated; row order cannot decide the network. The production Availity-to-Stedi fallback retains active Stedi benefits and a patient-identity comparison but does not promote the demo matrix to production contract evidence, because that matrix lacks the location and tax-ID scope of the Clinical Library contract. The V2 local estimator requires a bound patient before using member financial rows and rejects conflicting individual copay, coinsurance, deductible or remaining out-of-pocket rows rather than choosing the first returned value. A family deductible cannot fill an absent individual value. These V2 rules do not replace the scoped commercial estimator for a supported insured collection. Stedi explains that a 271 benefit-network indicator does not identify provider participation.

Card OCR before a payer request. For an eligible visit, the previsit check reads one patient-bound ClinicForce card set or, if none is saved, up to two eCW health-insurance-card attachments. It validates the files and runs OCR before the first Stedi eligibility or discovery request. Printed member name and birth date must agree with the eCW patient or linked subscriber before card details can be used. OCR fills only missing member ID, group number, payer name or matching subscriber birth date; it never overwrites eCW fields. A differing member ID or group, or multiple saved card sets, holds the new Stedi request for policy review. A differently worded payer name is shown for review while eCW remains the routing source. The estimate's card_check and Patient and policy panel show whether the card matched, filled fields, conflicted, was unavailable, or was not returned. If no usable card is available within the eight-second precheck, the estimate can continue with eCW details and still requires payer verification. Card image bytes are not added to the estimate response; safe card_precheck audit events record only outcome and file count.

Automatic coverage recovery and optional clearinghouses

The normal route resolves the known payer and uses available subscriber/member details first. A known payer and explicit subscriber identity may be queried with demographics when the member ID is absent; payer requirements vary. That response must return matching patient identity and a usable subscriber ID. Missing policy/routing, rejected identity and inactive coverage enter bounded background recovery. A patient-bound active medical 271 for the visit stops additional paid policy discovery; missing CPT, network, rate, deductible or collection-policy inputs belong to pricing or clinical review. No clinic-specific payer, identifier, benefit or price is invented.

Appointment-note candidates. A uniquely labeled payer and subscriber number in eCW General Notes can supply missing inquiry hints even when Coverage is empty. Candidates are extracted before checking policy count; conflicting notes or multiple active policies do not select a primary policy. The original note source is retained. Notes never establish an active policy, a self relationship, policyholder demographics, benefits or an amount. No returned General Notes means no note candidate; ClinicForce does not infer one from clinical narrative or patient comments.

Automatic member-ID recovery. No per-patient staff selection is required to finish recovery. First examine up to ten saved checks belonging to the same explicitly linked practice patient. One distinct policy can supply a candidate; current evidence is reused only with matching patient, provider, visit date, requested services, freshness and no newer eCW conflict. Older evidence requires a new eligibility check. Conflicting policies are never selected by recency. Saved case IDs and raw response references remain private.

  1. Read patient-bound ClinicForce cards or explicitly labeled eCW insurance cards through DocumentReference. ecw_scope_result records whether Coverage, DocumentReference and Binary read access was granted, without logging the token. Inline cards need only DocumentReference access; same-issuer Binary downloads obtain Binary access separately. Never forward credentials to other hosts or redirects. Up to two supported attachments are validated and read by the existing OCR pipeline. OCR supplies inquiry hints; only a matching payer response establishes benefits. Conflicting or unmatched card names cannot select a policy.
  2. After card recovery, a uniquely resolved eCW payer or labeled appointment-note payer can receive a member-ID or demographic inquiry even with zero Coverage records. Patient demographics in an inquiry do not assert a self relationship; the returned patient, subscriber, identifier and active coverage must match before adoption. Unverified directory suggestions are never automatically selected.
  3. If these sources do not resolve coverage, use Stedi discovery with available demographics, provider NPI and the appointment date. Send known subscriber/dependent identities separately. Preserve a known state without a usable ZIP. A completed no-match can try up to two different recorded prior addresses. Never invent an address or duplicate a request fingerprint in one recovery.
  4. Compare the returned patient name, exact DOB, relationship, address and active medical coverage for the service date. Normalize punctuation/case only; substantive identity differences require reconciliation. A missing eCW member ID is not required to recover a discovered ID. For a dependent, select the sole active, dated, verified policy automatically even when inactive policies are also returned. Count distinct medical policy identities independently of the number of discovery responses or benefit differences. Matching payer, member, group, product and dependent identity with different payer route identifiers is one policy; unrelated service categories and transport metadata do not split it. The staff suggestion dropdown groups agreeing medical copies and reports their source-response count, while the protected discovery envelope retains every original response. One fully verified copy may supply a matching duplicate that lacks only noncontradictory route or benefit detail; its requested visit-benefit rows must contain every row used from the duplicate, and both source references remain recorded. Conflicting patient, subscriber, address, coverage-date or visit-benefit evidence blocks automatic selection and positive collection without declaring a second medical policy. Different policy identities require payer-order review. Reuse an agreeing 271 when it includes the requested service categories; otherwise request service-specific eligibility after enrollment checks. Preserve both source responses and store the selected 271 separately from the discovery envelope so later recalculation reads the same evidence. A missing subscriber DOB is omitted, never replaced by a dependent's DOB.
  5. After unresolved discovery, a recorded Original Medicare Part B policy (MB) may use the documented MBILUNOSSN JSON lookup with patient name, DOB, state and billing-provider identity. It requires live CMS and MBI-lookup enrollment for that billing NPI; the application never enrolls or attests for the provider. Age alone, a dependent relationship, multiple existing policies or returned policies with unresolved ordering do not qualify. The lookup returns an MBI, not a Medicare Advantage plan member number. Its STC 30 response may verify coverage while specialist benefits remain unavailable. No direct state-Medicaid portal or additional-clearinghouse adapter is assumed. See Stedi MBI lookup and the HETS permitted-use rules. The lookup forwards the validated originating IP chain from the private request context in X-Forwarded-For; missing or invalid trace context skips the lookup. These addresses are never returned in public results or recovery logs. Production enablement must verify proxy-chain completeness, US network requirements, enrollment and attestation.
  6. Classify the payer-sourced medical product and exact eligibility route before checking enrollment. Traditional Original Medicare Part A/B routed to CMS HETS uses the billing NPI and returns a CMS HETS EDI attestation action when its enrollment is incomplete. Medicare Advantage uses its plan issuer's supported Stedi payer ID, including UHC KMQTZ for a valid Regional PPO response; BCBS and other insurers use their own payer-specific eligibility requirements. A CMS enrollment failure does not invalidate a different policy's saved, patient-bound Stedi response. The CMS Physician Fee Schedule is a separate reference source and does not establish eligibility.
  7. Price verified benefits using the practice's existing network and contracted-rate configuration. Preserve returned plan/group fields and original response evidence. Recovery does not activate rates or change eCW. Verified coverage can still lack a supported price.

recovery.coverage_status and the saved result's coverage_status use VERIFIED, REVIEW_REQUIRED and NOT_FOUND. NOT_FOUND means a completed empty search with no earlier active payer response, not uninsured. An earlier active, patient-bound 271 remains VERIFIED in the saved result even when a later search is empty or inconclusive; a returned active 271 with unresolved patient/subscriber identity remains REVIEW_REQUIRED. The recovery status describes only the later search and never overwrites the original eligibility state. recovery.state separately reports pending, completed, rejected or indeterminate processing; card_status and attempt_count expose fallback progress. Active payer benefits stay on the insurance path when an insured price is unavailable; clinic cash requires an explicit staff choice. When active medical coverage has not been established, completed recovery can still show a separately configured cash option unless insurance mode was chosen. recovery.exhausted is false while stages remain, and recovery.steps records safe stage outcomes including unavailable prerequisites. Pending or unknown payer transactions do not trigger a cash switch. Only complete configured practice cash prices support a cash amount; otherwise cash_price_missing identifies the missing schedule. Missing insured money remains null. Historical snapshots remain unchanged.

Terminal status. The coverage chip and summary use the same status. Only a pending recovery shows “Coverage check in progress”; saved snapshots say the check was pending. Completed empty searches show “Coverage not found electronically”, failed checks show “Coverage check could not complete”, indeterminate submissions show “Coverage check outcome unknown”, and other unresolved results require review. None means uninsured. A cached result with returned policies but no accepted identity match remains REVIEW_REQUIRED, not NOT_FOUND. Reopening a completed result does not submit another payer request. Missing benefits or prices remain unavailable even when coverage is verified.

recovery.payer_routes retains supported exact matches and up to five unverified directory suggestions when a payer name is not an exact match. Each shows name, payer ID, Stedi route, aliases, match basis and enrollment requirement. Staff can submit POST /cases/{case_id}/payer-route with the saved revision, context token and one returned route ID. This continues eligibility with known patient/subscriber details, including a demographic inquiry when the member ID is absent. Selection never asserts coverage, guesses identifiers, or changes eCW. Stale/arbitrary choices and pending/indeterminate transactions cannot trigger a second submission.

The optional eCW saved-eligibility read uses a separate CoverageEligibilityResponse.read token. A rejected OAuth scope or backend app returns a safe allowlisted reason code and an administrator action in ecw_saved_eligibility; it does not erase a separate active Stedi 271 response or turn missing eCW evidence into missing payer benefits. A prior HTTP 400 without an OAuth error code cannot establish which eCW configuration failed.

If those typed searches contain no usable card, it also searches the patient-only view, even when the patient-document search returned unrelated clinical documents. Only a current patient-bound image or PDF explicitly labeled as an insurance card is accepted. A patient-only query can return clinical documents without scanned cards. If eCW rejects a typed document filter with HTTP 400 or 422, the card reader still tries the remaining type and patient-only searches and accepts only a current patient-bound card; denied primary document access remains an access error. A missing eCW-card message remains visible when a ClinicForce upload is present. Check the practice build and API exposure with eCW. UHC card fallback. When the eCW health-insurance-card FHIR search returns no qualifying image for a UHC visit, the Patient and policy panel directs authorized staff with UHC Provider Portal access to open the member’s Eligibility record for a digital ID card, upload it to ClinicForce, and ask eCW to expose type 64290-0. ClinicForce has not integrated that direct API, so this fallback remains a staff portal action subject to clinic access and exact member/product/provider matching. The policy panel also tolerates older saved card-check snapshots without a fields_added list, so a missing eCW card does not break reopening the estimate.

Recovery eligibility and MBI attempts save their requests privately before submission; repeated eligibility request fingerprints are skipped. Discovery enrollment is checked read-only and missing enrollment is recorded as a limited search, because discovery does not require enrollment. Each discovery attempt saves its request privately before submission and retains the protected response. Background recovery has a nine-minute budget. Timeouts after submission remain indeterminate. Reading a saved result never submits another paid request; known pending discovery IDs can be polled. Automatic patient and subscriber matching

Clinicians do not enter policy corrections in ClinicForce; no correction form, write endpoint or manually maintained policy override is required. The normal previsit workflow reads Coverage?patient=[id], checks Coverage.beneficiary against the confirmed patient, and follows Coverage.subscriber to the linked Patient or RelatedPerson. An external resource must match the referenced type/ID; RelatedPerson must point back to this patient and must not be inactive. Unrelated contacts, guarantors and family names do not establish insurance membership. See eCW's Coverage specification and RelatedPerson specification. eCW documents patient coverage search and related-person reads by ID, not an enrolled-family roster API.

When a patient-bound card names a different subscriber, the existing OCR supplies inquiry hints. A different cardholder can be used when the complete card member ID matches the patient's single recorded policy and the subscriber is linked from eCW, or the printed name has an unambiguous two-part form with no conflicting group. Ambiguous names remain unresolved and the existing demographic discovery fallback continues. A linked subscriber whose first name differs from the patient is queried with the patient in the dependent slot even when eCW's self label is stale. No spouse/child relationship or unknown subscriber DOB is invented. Government ID code 53245-7 and superseded documents are excluded from insurance-card recovery. Card listing and included image reads request only system/DocumentReference.read; a separate Binary token is requested only when the linked image is absent from the Bundle. Failure of optional patient-document access does not discard a returned insurance card.

Stedi verifies the patient being treated; its eligibility API and insurance discovery are not treated as family-member directories. A patient with their own uniquely identifying member ID can be represented as subscriber for the transaction even when covered as a family dependent. Otherwise subscriber and dependent details stay separate. A subscriber-only response cannot verify a different dependent. An unknown subscriber DOB remains null and never inherits the patient's DOB.

Automatic resolution of a conflicting subscriber/relationship requires the returned dependent's matching first name/DOB, the same complete member ID as one active eCW policy, no conflicting returned group/plan, and corroboration from matching patient address, the patient-bound eCW subscriber link, or matching payer evidence. A one-edit surname discrepancy additionally requires matching full member ID and patient address; it remains a visible surname warning. Active medical benefits must apply to the service date; multiple active policies, conflicting identities, expired coverage and unknown request outcomes remain unresolved. A card alone cannot meet the benefit checks. The protected result's benefits.policy_match records differences resolved from dated payer evidence; original eCW inputs, saved source data and historical snapshots remain unchanged. Existing case-bound recovery and benefit-freshness checks control reuse. There is no manually maintained correction validity period. Unresolved policy conflicts continue through bounded automatic recovery. Saved candidate diagnostics are refreshed without issuing a new paid request.

The optional eCW saved-eligibility read requests only system/CoverageEligibilityResponse.read. The diagnostic probe first requests system/Patient.read to locate the exact patient, then requests system/CoverageEligibilityResponse.read and system/DocumentReference.read separately. A rejection of unrelated appointment scopes cannot stop the probe before it reports which optional resource is available. If the capability statement omits an optional resource, its status is not_advertised and the app does not request that resource token. If an advertised resource rejects its token request, its status is scope_denied_or_unavailable; a recognized OAuth error is returned as an allowlisted reason_code such as oauth_invalid_scope, without the raw provider body or token. The normal optional saved-eligibility reader likewise skips token signing when CoverageEligibilityResponse is absent from the capability statement, reports ecw_resource_not_advertised and continues to Stedi. When DocumentReference access works, the probe also makes a read-only 64290-0 insurance-card search and returns only the count of current patient-bound image/PDF attachments plus insurance_card_status: available, not_returned, no_readable_image, forbidden, not_supported_or_not_found or search_unavailable. It does not download Binary content or return card bytes. A generic DocumentReference count does not prove that an insurance-card image is exposed. A denied or unavailable entitlement still falls through to Stedi verification. This change does not grant missing eCW access: persistent token rejection requires the practice administrator or eCW to verify the registered entitlement, client, issuer and signing configuration. Failed retrieval is not absence of insurance. For an HTTP 400 token response, the safe log now records a fixed OAuth reason such as oauth_invalid_scope when eCW supplies that standard error; all other provider response text remains unlogged. A prior generic HTTP 400 alone cannot establish its OAuth cause.

Candidate retention, verification and diagnosis

Parsing and verification are independent. Every parseable discovery policy is retained, even with incomplete or mismatched identity. Exact duplicate items are combined; distinct policies remain separate. The authenticated, patient/practice-scoped recovery.candidates response includes candidate member ID, subscriber/dependent names and DOBs, payer, coverage dates, source, candidate state and verification reasons. These are protected patient details, never audit-log fields. Original benefits and raw evidence remain in private object storage; object keys and private request headers are not returned.

Missing subscriber DOB remains null. For dependent policies, match the dependent's name and DOB against the eCW patient; never substitute that DOB for the subscriber DOB. Missing address does not remove a candidate. Independent automatic verification requires matching normalized patient name/DOB, member ID and payer identifier, applicable dated active medical coverage, and corroborating address evidence. A conflicting relationship or minor surname typo can be resolved only by the additional evidence rules above. Existing eCW policy evidence can instead corroborate self or dependent coverage. Multiple active policies or insufficient evidence remain review-required. Candidate retention or staff selection alone never authorizes an insured estimate.

insurance_hints deduplicates identical payer/member clues from Coverage, notes and OCR while retaining their sources. Confirmed Coverage fields take precedence over unverified notes. Hints never establish active coverage. insurance_diagnosis is a ClinicForce contract, not Stedi's schema: coverageSource, rawCandidateCount, retainedCandidateCount, verifiedCandidateCount, identityStatus, eligibilityStatus, pricingStatus, reasonCodes, states and pricingReasons describe separate stages. Unreadable cards and unavailable prices

Card files are saved before OCR. For a card embedded in a PDF page, the reader OCRs the original card image rather than the page header and surrounding whitespace. It enlarges small images, recognizes both labeled three-column cards and separately labeled subscriber/identification/group fields, and tries alternate local OCR layouts when the first pass misses identity or payer details. A card read has a 20-second request deadline; if it times out, the saved original remains available for retry and manual entry. Each proposed field retains page evidence. Staff must compare every proposed name, birth date, member ID, group, and payer to the original card; an empty or uncertain field stays for manual entry. Many printed cards do not include date of birth. A card alone does not prove eligibility. A failed refresh preserves the same visit’s previous result and labels it Previous estimate · needs recheck, with its saved time and the error. It is not presented as a newly verified collection amount. Changing the visit clears that result. An initial failure without a prior result offers a visit/benefit retry and retains the missing-input explanation. Staff can retry the read, check the payer portal or call the payer, record the verification, and bill any final insured responsibility after adjudication. An arrival amount of $0.00 requires a supported calculation from applicable benefits and rates.

Only current, patient-bound documents labeled as insurance cards with a supported image or PDF attachment are listed. eCW lookup deadline

The staff patient lookup and commercial appointment list share an eCW reader. It limits metadata and backend authorization stages to 10 seconds each and the entire patient/appointment read to 24 seconds, ahead of CloudFront's 30-second origin read limit. On an upstream timeout, the API returns a retryable error instead of leaving the browser waiting for an edge 504. Timeout errors identify backend authorization, patient verification, or appointment search. The 24-second limit is ClinicForce’s request budget; reaching it alone does not establish an eCW outage or prove that a patient or appointment is absent. No appointment or eligibility result is inferred from a timed-out request. Use the response request ID and stage events described in the debugging section to distinguish a local deadline, transport failure, upstream HTTP rejection, and identity mismatch. Compare the request with the last working release before attributing an incident to an upstream service. Retry the read or review the saved card and verify benefits manually.

Why it is built this way

The browser handles the screen. The API handles permission and business rules. Stores keep the result. These boundaries make it easier to find and test a problem.

Keep secrets on the server

The browser uses a session cookie. Provider credentials stay in the backend.

Keep evidence with results

A saved case is work in progress. A completed payer response is evidence.

Keep identities separate

Portal patients and clinic patients have different access rules.

Release the same build

Details and source links

This section explains the design implemented in this repository and the engineering reasons it supports. It is not a reconstructed meeting history: where no recorded decision exists, the rationale is an interpretation of the current code and constraints, not a claim that every alternative was evaluated. Current limitations are called out rather than presented as ideal design. Use the linked files to verify a decision before changing it.

Browser → API → rules → stores

Reason and behavior. The browser sends intent; the server validates identity, clinic scope, input and workflow state before accessing data or providers. Separating route contracts from insurance logic lets calculation rules be tested without a browser or real payer.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. Direct browser-to-database/provider calls would distribute credentials and authorization into an untrusted client. A microservice for every layer would add deployment and network boundaries; the current single backend is simpler to coordinate, but shares scaling and failure capacity. Split services when independently measured load or ownership justifies that cost.

Code to open: backend/app/api/application.py (route assembly), backend/app/api/routers/commercial_estimator.py (workflow orchestration), backend/app/insurance/visit_estimate.py (pricing rules).

Same-origin sessions instead of browser-held provider tokens

Reason and behavior. HttpOnly cookies let the app and Swagger reuse the same server-validated login without exposing session tokens to JavaScript. The backend holds eCW/Stedi credentials; mutation-origin checks complement cookie authentication.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. Browser bearer tokens require their own storage/refresh and exposure controls. Cookies require careful CSRF/origin protection and environment isolation; HttpOnly does not make XSS harmless. Logging in to staging does not log you in to production. A share/PIN token is a separate resource grant, not a clinic account.

Code to open: backend/app/api/services/authentication.py (patient checks), backend/app/api/routers/staff.py (clinic checks), frontend/src/services/api/client.ts (same-origin requests).

Public API reference, login required to execute

Reason and behavior. Readers can inspect every contract without an account. Swagger enables matching patient or clinician operations only after main-app sign-in, and checks the session again before execution. A server-side Swagger marker gate rejects missing/wrong sessions; the actual route still checks ownership and permissions.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. A completely read-only viewer prevented useful authenticated testing. A separate developer login would duplicate identity management. Swagger now performs real writes and potentially paid provider calls: use authorized test inputs in staging. Removing the Swagger marker does not grant access to a protected API; it restores that route’s normal public/authenticated contract.

Code to open: backend/app/api/developer_docs.py (audience metadata and server gate), backend/app/api/developer_docs.js (browser session checks and intake context).

Separate portal and clinic patient identities

Reason and behavior. A clinic can receive a card and confirm a patient who has never registered for the portal. Practice-owned staff_patients and staff_intakes therefore differ from portal patients and their sessions. eCW identity matching is an explicit reconciliation step.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. One shared patient ID would simplify joins but incorrectly equate portal registration, clinic ownership and an external EHR identifier. Separation needs explicit links and identity confirmation; name/DOB ambiguity cannot be solved by choosing the first search hit.

Code to open: backend/app/core/staff_store.py (clinic identities), backend/app/core/patient_store.py (portal identities), backend/app/insurance/ecw_backend.py (external identity checks).

PostgreSQL rows, S3 files, HAPI FHIR

Reason and behavior. Relational rows support accounts, ownership, case state, joins and rate versions. S3 holds original documents and raw response evidence, with rows keeping references. HAPI provides a FHIR resource interface instead of making every caller implement a FHIR server.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. Putting all bytes in PostgreSQL expands backups and mixes file delivery with row queries. Using only object storage makes transactional relationships harder. These stores are not one atomic transaction: missing objects and orphan cleanup need explicit handling. Cloud HAPI and application tables currently share one database and role, reducing isolation; separate databases/roles would strengthen that boundary at additional operational cost.

Code to open: backend/app/core/patient_store.py (row schemas), backend/app/core/object_store.py (object keys and encryption), backend/app/ingestion/fhir_client.py (FHIR client).

eCW supplies visit context; Stedi supplies payer evidence

Reason and behavior. The eCW reader resolves patient identity and visit/coverage/provider context. Stedi eligibility/discovery is a distinct payer interaction. Clinic rate tables and benefit interpretation then produce an estimate; a successful eligibility response alone does not contain a guaranteed patient price.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. A single “insurance result” abstraction would hide whether a fact came from the EHR, payer or clinic. Explicit evidence/source/time and identity checks require more reconciliation but avoid treating unknown coverage as zero cost. Revisit an adapter when a provider contract changes, preserving source provenance and failure states.

Code to open: backend/app/insurance/ecw_backend.py (FHIR reader), backend/app/insurance/stedi.py (eligibility client), backend/app/insurance/stedi_discovery.py (coverage discovery), backend/app/insurance/commercial_store.py (rate/evidence selection), backend/app/insurance/visit_estimate.py (estimate calculation).

Persist pending state and reuse qualifying evidence

Reason and behavior. The workflow saves submission state before a payer request and retains case/event/evidence records. Matching previously obtained evidence can avoid repeat calls, but patient, practice, payer, dates and workflow context must qualify it. Events retain historical snapshots alongside mutable current state.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. Always calling the payer increases latency, cost and duplicate-submission risk; caching indefinitely can return stale or unrelated evidence. A timeout does not prove the remote action failed. There is no claim of an atomic transaction spanning PostgreSQL, S3 and the payer, or universal exactly-once delivery. Inspect saved state before retrying.

Code to open: backend/app/api/routers/commercial_estimator.py (submission ordering), backend/app/insurance/store.py (cases and events), backend/app/insurance/commercial_store.py (evidence reuse).

Bounded synchronous eCW lookup

Reason and behavior. The staff interaction waits for the reader with a 24-second overall budget and a 26-second outer route deadline, ahead of CloudFront’s 30-second origin read timeout. This permits an application error and support reference rather than an edge-generated HTML timeout.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. Longer waits cannot repair an unresponsive upstream and may exceed the edge deadline. A background job/polling design could support longer work but needs job identity, status storage, cancellation, deduplication and a different UI. Current failure is explicit: no patient/appointment absence or successful estimate is inferred from a timeout.

Code to open: backend/app/insurance/ecw_backend.py (reader deadlines), backend/app/api/routers/staff.py (route deadline).

Managed AWS services with explicit network boundaries

Reason and behavior. CloudFront serves cached static assets and routes API requests to ALB/ECS Fargate. RDS is private; object buckets block public access. Fargate removes host management while security groups restrict service-to-service ingress.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. EC2 would require host patching/capacity management. Kubernetes would add orchestration overhead for this small service set. The current design is not maximum isolation or availability: tasks use public subnets/IPs for outbound access, no NAT is declared, service desired counts are one, and some internal hops use HTTP. Private compute, internal TLS and multiple tasks are improvements to assess against availability/security needs and cost—not assumed existing protections.

Code to open: stacks/clinicforce-production.yaml; see the deployed topology and storage map.

KMS signing and separate credential storage

Reason and behavior. An asymmetric KMS key signs eCW app assertions without exporting the private key. Secrets Manager supplies server credentials at task startup; the browser receives neither provider secrets nor signing keys.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. A private key file is easier locally but creates key-distribution and rotation obligations in production. KMS adds IAM permissions, network dependency and signing latency. Injected secrets are not automatically refreshed in a running task; rotation requires task replacement where configured.

Code to open: backend/app/ingestion/ecw.py (assertion signing), backend/app/insurance/ecw_backend.py (token exchange).

Build once, stage, then promote exact artifacts

Reason and behavior. CI checks source, builds image digests/frontend bytes, deploys staging and verifies that artifact. Production promotion reuses those exact bytes so the released code is the code tested in staging. Application and infrastructure repositories keep image releases separate from resource provisioning.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. Rebuilding for production can resolve different dependencies or base images. Direct local uploads lose provenance. Artifact reuse depends on retention, compatible database changes and preserved image digests; rollback is not a database restore. Two repositories also mean coordinating stack updates and documentation explicitly.

Code to open: .github/workflows/deploy.yml (CI/CD jobs), scripts/check_release.py (provenance checks), scripts/release.py (artifact deployment).

Structured request diagnostics without payload logging

Reason and behavior. Request IDs and fixed stage/resource labels correlate browser errors with upstream timing and application outcomes. CloudWatch stores events; CloudTrail records AWS actions such as signing. Patient values, credentials and raw provider bodies are excluded from the structured diagnostic events.

Alternatives and tradeoffs. Dumping complete requests is easier for ad hoc debugging but exposes sensitive data. Reduced logs cannot reveal eCW’s internal cause or reproduce patient data; private source-system investigation may still be needed. Audit current legacy logging separately instead of assuming every log stream follows the structured schema.

Code to open: backend/app/logs/collection_audit.py (safe event fields), backend/app/api/application.py (request correlation), backend/app/insurance/ecw_backend.py (upstream stages).
